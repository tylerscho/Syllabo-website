Privacy policy

Last updated 13 November 2026

Syllabo keeps your study data on your device. There is no advertising and no tracking. The app sends our server anonymous usage counts, such as how many people finish setup each day, with nothing that identifies you; you can turn them off, and Usage counts and feedback below lists what they contain. If you create an online account, your username, name, email and a backup of your study data are stored with our database provider, Supabase, so you can sign in on another phone and add friends. When AI is turned on, what you send to AI marking or the tutor goes to Anthropic through our server.

Your account

Creating an account stores your name, email address, username and a salted hash of your password on this device only. We cannot see it, reset it or recover it. If you enable it, Face ID or Touch ID can be used to reset a forgotten password on the same device. Guest mode stores no identifying details. Deleting your account in the app removes it and all of its study data permanently.

Online accounts (optional)

An online account stores your name, email address and username with Supabase, and your password is handled by Supabase Authentication; we never see it in plain text. A backup of your study data (subjects, progress, buddy, preferences and saved questions) is kept with your account so it can be restored when you sign in. Friends see only the summary your privacy settings allow. Deleting your account in the app deletes it online and on your phone. Guest accounts stay on your device only.

Study reminders

Daily reminders are scheduled on your phone by iOS; nothing about them is sent to us. Turn them off or change their times in Settings › Reminders.

Signing in with Apple or Google

If you continue with Apple or Google, they confirm who you are and share your name and email address (with Apple you can choose to hide your email, and Apple gives us a private relay address instead). We never see your Apple or Google password. When you delete an account that uses Sign in with Apple, Syllabo asks Apple to remove its access to your Apple ID.

What stays on your device

  • Your subjects, school units, notes, exams, saved-question folders and imported past-paper questions
  • Mastery, question attempts, mistakes, sessions, XP, coins, streaks and your study buddy
  • Your friends list, requests and privacy settings
  • Appearance, typeface and Review-lock preferences, and the list of apps you chose to lock
  • An API key you choose to enter for your own AI provider, kept in the iOS Keychain

Deleting the app removes all of it. There is no copy anywhere else, other than the backup kept with an online account. None of it is in the anonymous usage counts described below.

AI marking and tutor

When you allow AI, answer marking, diagram marking, examiner and essay feedback, Teach the buddy, the Tutor tab and making flashcards from your notes send your answer, drawing, message or note text, the question and its mark scheme, and your first name, subject, level, current syllabus point and short mastery and recent-mistake summaries to Anthropic's Claude models through Syllabo's server (a Supabase Edge Function), over encrypted connections. This works with an online account, or as a guest, with a smaller daily allowance, through an anonymous sign-in with no name or email. Our server does not keep what you send. It records how many AI requests an account made each day, to apply a daily allowance, and, to stop abuse, counts requests per minute against a one-way hash of your network (IP) address, which is cleared within about an hour. Anthropic handles the data under its own privacy policy. The first time you use AI the app asks for your permission, and you can turn AI off at any time in Settings › AI marking and tutor. If you instead enter your own Claude or Gemini API key, the same data goes directly to that provider under its own terms. With AI off, or without a connection, the offline tutor and meaning-based marking work on your device and nothing is sent.

Importing your notes

When you import class notes, the camera scan, photos or PDF pages are turned into text on your device by Apple's text recognition; Syllabo does not upload or keep the images. Word and PowerPoint files, and files you send to Syllabo from other apps, are read on your device too. You can edit the text before anything is made from it. Offline, flashcards are made on your device. With AI on, the note text (never the images) is sent as described under AI marking and tutor to make flashcards and questions. What you save, including the note text so more questions can be made from it later, stays on your device, and with an online account it is included in your backup. Deleting an import deletes its text too.

Friends

Friends are added by username; with online accounts they can be on any phone. A friend only sees what you allow: activity, mastery and streak can each be hidden, and you can turn off discovery by username. Friends never see your mistakes, notes, answers or email address.

Quick Add and finding friends from contacts

Quick Add suggests people you may know: friends of your friends (with the names of friends you share) and people you have played a duel with. Only students who leave Discoverable on are suggested.

If you tap Find friends from your contacts, Syllabo asks for access to your contacts and, on your phone, turns each contact's email address and phone number into a one-way code (a SHA-256 hash; a phone number is hashed from its last 8 digits). Only those codes are sent, they are compared with students who chose to be found that way, and they are not stored. Matches show the name saved in your own contacts, which never leaves your phone.

You can be found by people who have your email only if you turn on Friends › Find me from contacts › By my email, and by your phone number only if you add it there. We keep only the hashed code of your number, and you can remove it at any time.

Leagues and duels

With an online account you are placed each week in a league of up to 30 students at a similar level, who see your username, display name and XP for that week. In a duel, your friend sees your score, and how you did on each question once you have both played. Scores, results and the coins involved are stored on our server to settle leagues and duels, with a short record of them kept to prevent cheating.

Study buddies

A study pact is made with a friend who has an online account. For it, our server stores the pact (the two of you, its length and when it started) and the dates on which you studied: a date only, never what you studied. Your buddy can see the pact's progress and whether you have studied today. Study dates are deleted after about 400 days, and ending a pact stops it for both of you.

App updates

To tell you when a new version is out, the app asks Apple's App Store which version is current; that request contains no personal information. Requests to our server include the app's version number, so that very old versions can be asked to update.

Usage counts and feedback

To see where new students get stuck and what breaks, the app sends our server anonymous usage counts. They are on by default. You can turn them off in Settings › Privacy › Share anonymous usage counts, which also empties any counts waiting to be sent. This is what they record:

  • The welcome screen and each setup step reached, going back a step or leaving setup, and when setup finishes: how long it took, how many subjects, how many at HL, your daily minutes and how many subjects had topics skipped. An AP® setup also says it was AP®
  • Sessions started and ended: the kind of session, its planned minutes and questions, how many you answered, how long it took, whether you finished, and the reason you gave if you ended early
  • For your first 7 days after installing, each question answered: its type, whether it was right, partly right or wrong, how it was marked, the number of hints, and whether you gave up or changed the mark. Never the question or your answer. Questions skipped, and why
  • The first time you open each tab, the app being opened again, and a tour being skipped
  • Choices about saving your progress, creating an account and how (for example email or Apple), allowing AI, reminders, the widget offer and school leagues
  • Things that failed, as short codes: AI marking, sign-in, saving, the App Store page
  • How many times the app crashed or froze, as reported by iOS. Only the number, never the crash report itself

Each count also carries the app version, whether it is a release or test build, the kind of device (iPhone, iPad or Mac), the iOS major version and the number of days since the app was installed. The first session count also carries the subject's short name. The app sends the time to the minute and our server stores it to the hour. No count carries your name, email, account, a device or advertising identifier, or anything you typed.

Counts reach our server over the internet, so your network (IP) address is seen as the request arrives. To stop abuse, requests are counted per minute against a one-way keyed hash of that address, cleared within about an hour. The address is not stored with the counts. Counts are used only to improve the app, never for advertising, and are not shared. They are kept for 180 days. Our server can also turn them off for everyone.

If you send feedback from Settings › Send feedback, Report this question or the short How was that? card, we receive it only when you tap Send: what you typed, the question and your own answer if you include them, version and device details, and the names of the last few things you did in the app so we can see what led to the problem. If you are signed in, the feedback is linked to your account so we can reply; as a guest it is anonymous unless you give an email address for a reply. Replies come from syllabosupport@gmail.com. Feedback is kept until it is dealt with, and for at most two years; an email address given for a reply is removed after 90 days. Deleting your account removes its link to any feedback you sent. The counts and feedback are stored on our Supabase server in Tokyo, Japan.

Microphone and speech

Teach-the-buddy mode can turn your voice into text with Apple's speech recognition, only while the microphone is on (tap it to start and stop). On iOS 26 and later it uses Apple's on-device model, so the audio stays on your device. Where that model isn't on the device yet, or if you choose “Apple's servers” in Teach setup, the audio is sent to Apple to be transcribed when you're online, as with keyboard dictation, and transcribed on your device when you're offline. The lesson's key terms are passed to the recognizer as hints, and subject words it hears slightly wrong are corrected on your device. Audio is not stored by Syllabo. Replies read aloud (in Teach the buddy, and in the tutor if you switch it on) use Apple's voices on your device or, if you download it, the buddy's own voice, which also runs on your device: no text is sent anywhere to be spoken. That voice is downloaded once, when you agree, from Hugging Face, which sees your network (IP) address as any website does; nothing else about you is sent.

What the app does not do

  • No tracking across apps or websites, and no usage data that identifies you
  • No advertising or advertising identifiers
  • No third-party analytics, trackers or SDKs
  • No selling or sharing of personal information
  • No servers holding your study data unless you choose an online account; the anonymous usage counts contain none of it

Children

The app is designed for IB Diploma and AP® students managing their own study. It does not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13.
