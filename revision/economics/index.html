IB Economics revision for HL and SL

Practise IB Economics with an elasticity calculation, a subsidy question and worked marking points.

What to revise

Start with markets, then apply the same habit to macroeconomics and the global economy: identify what changed, show the mechanism, and explain who is affected. For HL, practise calculations and use the result to support a judgement about policy.

Practice questions

Price elasticity of demand · 3 marks

The price of a cinema ticket falls from $10 to $9. Using the original figures as your bases, quantity demanded rises from 200 to 230 tickets per week. Calculate the price elasticity of demand and interpret its magnitude.

Show marking points
  1. Quantity demanded rises by 15% and price falls by 10%.
  2. PED = 15 ÷ (−10) = −1.5. A magnitude of 1.5 is equivalent here.
  3. Demand is price elastic because the magnitude exceeds 1. Quantity demanded changes by a larger percentage than price.

Producer subsidy · 3 marks

A government pays firms a subsidy for each bicycle they sell. In a competitive bicycle market with downward-sloping demand and upward-sloping supply, explain how the subsidy affects the price paid by buyers and the equilibrium quantity.

Show marking points
  1. The subsidy lowers the marginal cost net of subsidy, shifting supply down and to the right.
  2. The new intersection with the demand curve is at a lower price paid by buyers.
  3. The new intersection is at a higher equilibrium quantity.

Two mistakes to check

  • Elasticity divides percentage changes. Dividing 30 tickets by one dollar measures something different.
  • On a subsidy diagram, identify the buyer's price and the producer's price including the subsidy. The vertical gap is the per-unit subsidy.
