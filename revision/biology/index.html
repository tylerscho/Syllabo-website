IB Biology revision for HL and SL

Practise IB Biology with two original questions, marking points and advice on writing explanations and interpreting data.

What to revise

Work across four scales: molecules, cells, whole organisms and ecosystems. Pair recall with application. After revising membrane transport, explain a change in cell mass. After revising ecosystems, interpret a graph and identify what the data actually measured.

Practice questions

Osmosis in potato tissue · 3 marks

A student places equal-sized potato cylinders in distilled water for 40 minutes, blots them dry and finds that their mass has increased. Explain the increase in mass.

Show marking points
  1. The distilled water initially has a higher water potential than the potato cell contents.
  2. Water moves down the water potential gradient through partially permeable cell membranes by osmosis.
  3. Net water uptake increases the mass of the cylinders.

Light and primary production · 3 marks

In two bottles of pond water, dissolved oxygen starts at 7.0 mg/L. After one hour it is 8.4 mg/L in the light bottle and 6.6 mg/L in the dark bottle. Calculate the gross rate of oxygen production, assuming respiration remains the same in both bottles.

Show marking points
  1. In the light, net production is 8.4 − 7.0 = 1.4 mg/L per hour.
  2. In the dark, oxygen consumption by respiration is 7.0 − 6.6 = 0.4 mg/L per hour.
  3. Gross production is 1.4 + 0.4 = 1.8 mg/L per hour.

Two mistakes to check

  • With osmosis, name the movement of water and give the direction in terms of water potential. “Particles moved from high concentration to low concentration” leaves both unclear.
  • A change in oxygen in the light is a net measurement. Account for respiration before calling it gross production.
